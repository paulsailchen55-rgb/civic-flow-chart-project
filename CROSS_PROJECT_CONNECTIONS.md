# Cross-Project Connections

The Civic Flow Chart is the broader civic navigation and accountability framework. Related research projects remain independently maintained so that their specialized questions, evidence, prototypes, and risks do not get absorbed into the civic map.

## 1. School Technology & Children's Well-Being

Repository: `paulsailchen55-rgb/school-technology-children-wellbeing`

This project studies school technology, device design, classroom environments, accessibility, privacy, procurement, lifecycle economics, and children's well-being.

The Civic Flow Chart can provide a method for mapping the civic side of that work, including:

- which school, city, county, state, federal, or other authority is responsible for a question;
- how procurement and technology decisions move through institutions;
- what evidence is required at different process nodes;
- where residents, students, families, educators, and researchers can provide information;
- what is current policy versus proposed change;
- what clocks, costs, reviews, appeals, and decision points apply.

The school technology repository remains the home for the substantive technology research and design questions.

## 2. Anonymized Conversation & Evidence Exchange

Repository: `paulsailchen55-rgb/anonymized-conversation-evidence`

This project studies privacy-preserving sharing of conversations, AI interactions, recordings, documents, and other digital material while considering provenance, verification, security, accessibility, and accountability.

The Civic Flow Chart may eventually use these methods when a resident feedback or evidence path needs to preserve privacy without losing necessary provenance and context.

The evidence-exchange repository remains the home for anonymization, provenance, audit, re-identification, and security design questions.

## 3. Relationship rule

These projects should be connected by **cross-reference, not absorption**.

A useful architecture is:

**SPECIALIZED PROJECTS → CROSS-PROJECT REFERENCES → CIVIC FLOW CHART**

The Civic Flow Chart should not become the authority over the specialized research. It provides navigation, process mapping, evidence-routing, and public-access structure.

## 4. Shared evidence discipline

The projects share a basic distinction among:

**FACT · MODEL · ESTIMATE · PERSONAL OBSERVATION · HYPOTHESIS · PROPOSAL · OPINION**

They should also preserve uncertainty rather than manufacture completeness:

**UNKNOWN / NOT YET MAPPED**

When sources disagree:

**DISPUTED**

The map can make a disagreement inspectable without deciding the disagreement merely because it has been mapped.

## 5. Project Map / Crosswalk

The three repositories can eventually be summarized by a small project map:

| Project | Primary purpose | What it contributes to the others |
| --- | --- | --- |
| School Technology & Children's Well-Being | Research and design questions about children's interaction with school technology | Domain evidence, questions, experiments, procurement/lifecycle research |
| Anonymized Conversation & Evidence Exchange | Privacy-preserving sharing and evidence provenance | Privacy, provenance, transformation, verification, and security methods |
| Civic Flow Chart | Human-readable civic process and accountability map | Authority, responsibility, routing, clocks, friction, correction, archive, public access |

This crosswalk should remain lightweight. Its purpose is to prevent duplication and lost connections, not to create another management system.

## Working rule

**Keep the projects separate. Make the connections visible.**
