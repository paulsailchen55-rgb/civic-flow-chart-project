# Civic Flow Chart Project — Change Log

This file records substantive changes to the public working specification.

## 2026-09-17 — Resident feedback and evidence bridge

### Added

- A **Resident Feedback and Evidence Bridge** to connect observations made during use of a civic process with the organization responsible for operating, administering, reviewing, or making decisions about the relevant part of that system.
- A distinction between **in-the-moment observation** and **later reflection**.
- A traceable feedback pathway:
  **REPORT → ROUTE → RECEIPT → STATUS → USE/REVIEW**.
- Explicit distinction between the person or office receiving a report and the person or institution with decision authority.
- Confirmation by accessible channels such as email, text, printed receipt, or another appropriate method, where technically and legally appropriate.
- Individual-case versus aggregate-evidence distinctions.
- Privacy classes separating private case information, restricted information, public aggregates, and public source evidence.
- Provenance requirements for feedback so immediate observations are not silently replaced by later summaries.
- Feedback-system self-audit questions, including whether the feedback channel itself becomes friction or a new gatekeeper.

### Design principle

> **People should be able to reach the people responsible for the system, and the people responsible for the system should be able to see the evidence produced by its use.**

### Boundary

The feedback bridge routes and preserves evidence. It does not itself determine policy outcomes, establish legal authority, or guarantee that a reported problem will result in a change.

## Earlier Version 1 baseline

Version 1 established the core vocabulary and framework, including nodes, clocks, friction, authority and responsibility, backtracking, evidence distinctions, Resident/Government/Flow views, Ledger/Lab/Archive, civic memory/continuity/renewal, maintenance and uncertainty, self-audit, and the separation of current process from proposed alternatives.
